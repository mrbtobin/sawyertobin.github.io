# Grade placement and age suitability, K–5

## Sources and scope

California's [Common Core State Standards: English Language Arts and Literacy](https://www.cde.ca.gov/be/st/ss/documents/finalelaccssstandards.pdf) include the national Common Core standards plus California additions. See Reading Standards for Literature K–5 (printed pages 11–13), Reading Standards for Informational Text K–5, and “Standard 10: Range, Quality, and Complexity of Student Reading K–5” (printed page 41). The latter describes three components: qualitative features, quantitative measures, and the reader and task. California's added material is identified as “CA” in that document. These learning standards describe skills and text complexity; they do **not** prescribe a list of acceptable or prohibited story content or assign every title a grade.

The national [Common Core ELA standards](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/) are the corresponding national reference; follow California's adopted document for California additions. These are English-language literacy standards: Spanish texts require review by a qualified Spanish-language educator and should not be assigned solely by applying English readability formulas. Check the current district's adopted materials, accessibility and inclusion policies; local review and legal requirements may differ. The following content guidance is Shellfie's editorial policy, not a direct quotation of a standard.

## Placement workflow

1. Identify the exact edition, language, provenance and rights. A public-domain original does not automatically make a later translation or adaptation public domain. Record the evidence and jurisdiction. For a copyrighted or rights-uncertain requested book, create a clearly labeled original story on a general theme if appropriate; do not copy protected characters, expression, or distinctive plot, and do not present it as the requested book or an authorized adaptation.
2. Review the complete story, illustrations when present, and all quiz questions and distractors. Assess plot, structure, themes, language, prior knowledge and cultural context, as well as sentence/word complexity and the kind of support a reader will receive. Consider the actual quiz tasks separately from the prose.
3. Use a suitable quantitative measure if available, alongside—not instead of—qualitative and reader/task review. Record the measure and edition; do not invent a score or claim a grade from page length, age or a score alone. Grades 2–3 and 4–5 are complexity bands in RL.10, with scaffolding at the high end; K and grade 1 are described separately. Follow the full standards for instructional expectations.
4. Assign or reassign a **provisional** grade when review supports a better fit, document the previous grade, reasons, evidence and any support needed, and get educator review before claiming alignment or school approval. A grade is a shelf recommendation, not a restriction on individual students. Escalate uncertain rights, sources, historical context and challenging content.

| Grade | Starting point for text and quiz review, not a rigid reading formula |
| --- | --- |
| K | Short, clear stories and familiar sequences; with prompting/support, identify characters, events and key details. Consider illustrations or adult support. |
| 1 | Clear sequence and accessible vocabulary; ask about key details, retell, and describe characters, settings and major events. |
| 2 | Increasingly connected passages; ask who/what/where/when/why/how, recount and identify a lesson; complexity band 2–3 with support as needed. |
| 3 | More developed plots and vocabulary; use text details to explain events and character actions, and identify a central message; band 2–3. |
| 4 | More complex structure and themes; refer to details and examples, describe characters and explain inferences; band 4–5 with support as needed. |
| 5 | More layered themes and relationships; quote accurately, compare characters or events and explain themes; band 4–5. |

These summaries are practical starting points based on RL.1–3 and RL.10, not the complete standards. Consider informational texts and their corresponding RI standards where applicable. Do not equate more mature subject matter with educational value or reading difficulty.

## Shellfie editorial content guidance

- **All grades:** avoid graphic descriptions or images of injury, mutilation, torture and killing; sexual exploitation or explicit sexual content; instructions for self-harm or dangerous acts; and dehumanizing slurs or stereotypes. Review historical language in its full context. Do not automatically treat the existence of racial, religious, disability, LGBTQ+ or other identities, or respectful accounts of families and traditions, as inappropriate. Protect student privacy.
- **K–1:** prefer reassuring resolution and mild, brief conflict. Avoid realistic threats, detailed cruelty, and frightening depictions. Loss or death, if included, should be non-graphic, handled sensitively and reviewed by an educator for age and context.
- **2–3:** mild peril or conflict can be considered with context and resolution. Review non-graphic death, bullying, weapons, fear, and historical injustice carefully; avoid prolonged or intense threat and do not normalize cruelty.
- **4–5:** more complex historical or social conflict and non-graphic death may be considered when educationally purposeful and handled sensitively. Review violence, weapons, prejudice and trauma in context, avoiding sensationalism and graphic detail. Higher grade does not waive the all-grade rules.
- **Quizzes:** review the stem, evidence, correct answer and every distractor for suitability, accuracy, age fit, clarity and support from the revised text. An option should not introduce a disturbing scene absent from the text. Check translations separately.

For every concern, preserve the original in version history; record exact location, source, change, reason and relevant guidance in `books/REVISION_LOG.md`. For public-domain material, clearly label material changes as adaptations and preserve attribution; do not misrepresent altered history as a faithful quotation. The current user has authorized revisions with a log for later review. Do not assume that authorization establishes copyright permission for an unverified source.
